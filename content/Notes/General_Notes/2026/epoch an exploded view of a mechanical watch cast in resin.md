Fredrik Ellertsen spent 2.5 years figuring out how to build an exploded view of a mechanical watch — a real movement suspended in clear epoxy resin so you can hold it and see every component in its relative position. Inspired by Bartosz Ciechanowski's interactive web illustration of a mechanical watch.

The problem: nobody makes these. Watchmakers assemble watches, they don't "explode" them into art. He had to invent the process from scratch.

**Prototyping journey:**
- Attempt 1: UV resin, layer by layer — yellow tint, visible seams, impossible to do 20 layers
- Attempt 2: Silicone mold, cast thin epoxy disks and stack them — messy, bubbles, seam problems
- Attempt 3: single monolithic pour using monofilament fishing line to suspend components — index of refraction matches epoxy, becomes nearly invisible. Baked the fishing line to straighten it
- Attempt 4: PT5000 (ETA 2824-2 clone) movement — paint dissolved in epoxy, model came out crooked from vacuum chamber issues
- Final: spray lacquer to seal painted surfaces, better jig for cutting fishing line. Result: a wristwatch exploded view cast in clear resin

**Technical details:** Uses 0.7mm monofilament nylon leader. CA glue applied with pinhead (mirrors watchmaker oiling technique). Vacuum chamber to degas bubbles. The fishing line's index of refraction is close enough to cured epoxy that you can't see it.

**My take:** This is peak maker content — a guy who saw something cool, realized nobody had made the physical version, and spent 2.5 years failing forward until he got it right. The problem-solving chain (fishing line → CA glue instead of oil → spray lacquer for paint sealing) parallels real watchmaking technique. The result looks like a museum display piece made by someone who taught themselves everything along the way.
