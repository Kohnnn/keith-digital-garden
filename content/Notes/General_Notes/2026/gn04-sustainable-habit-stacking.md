# gn04 sustainable habit stacking beyond the 21 day myth

The "21 days to form a habit" figure is not science — it's a Maxwell Maltz plastic surgery observation from the 1960s that got repurposed into self-help dogma. The actual research (Lally et al., 2009) found 18 to 254 days depending on the behavior and context. I've been tracking my own habit adherence and what I found is that environment design beats willpower by a wide margin.

## key idea
Context-dependent stacking means attaching a new habit to an existing routine trigger rather than relying on motivation or calendar discipline. The trigger is the anchor — not the date.

## notes
- The Lally study showed missed days don't destroy habit formation; consistency of context matters more
- Identity-based framing ("I'm the kind of person who...") outperforms outcome-based goals
- Friction reduction for good habits, friction addition for bad ones — this is the single highest-leverage intervention
- The 2-minute rule: scale the habit down to a version that takes <2 minutes to start
