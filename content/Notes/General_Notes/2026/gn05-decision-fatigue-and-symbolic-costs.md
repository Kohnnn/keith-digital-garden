# gn05 decision fatigue and the symbolic cost of context switching

I noticed that my worst decisions happen not when I'm tired but when I've context-switched too many times. The research backs this up: task-switching costs 20-40% of productive time, and the perceived "mental tax" of switching is underestimated by most people by a factor of 2x.

## key idea
Symbolic costs are the invisible overhead of shifting between mental models. Every switch burns glucose and attentional budget that doesn't recover instantly. Batch-processing similar tasks reduces the symbolic load.

## notes
- The Zeigarnik effect means unfinished tasks occupy working memory — switching before completion increases cognitive load
- Time-blocking is effective primarily because it reduces switching frequency, not because of the schedule itself
- The default mode network needs idle time to consolidate — constant switching prevents that
- My rule: no more than 3 context domains per work session
