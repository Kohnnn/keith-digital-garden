# gn06 learning loops and feedback systems

Experience alone does not produce expertise. What separates growth from repetition is the feedback loop: act, observe, adjust, repeat. Without structured feedback, ten years of practice is just one year repeated ten times. The research on deliberate practice (Ericsson, 1993) makes this clear — the driver of improvement is not hours logged but the quality of the feedback cycle.

## key idea
A learning loop requires three components: a clear objective, immediate feedback on performance, and the ability to adjust the next iteration. Missing any one of these breaks the loop.

## notes
- Deliberate practice requires tasks at the edge of current ability, not comfortable repetition
- Feedback must be specific and timely — vague or delayed feedback is indistinguishable from no feedback
- The gap between action and reflection is where bad habits compound
- Writing market notes in public is a learning loop: thesis, outcome, review
- The digital garden structure supports this by making outputs inspectable over time
