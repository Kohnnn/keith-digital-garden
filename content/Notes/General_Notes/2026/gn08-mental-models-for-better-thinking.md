# gn08 mental models for better thinking

A mental model is a simplified representation of how something works. The more models you have, the better you see the full shape of a problem. Charlie Munger called it a "latticework of mental models" — the idea that no single discipline contains enough tools to handle real-world complexity.

## key idea
A single model blinds you to everything it cannot explain. The engineer sees structure. The economist sees incentives. The psychologist sees bias. The truth of any situation usually lives in the overlap.

## models I return to
- **Inversion** — assume the opposite and ask how to achieve it
- **Occam's razor** — the simplest explanation is more likely to be correct
- **Hanlon's razor** — do not attribute to malice what can be explained by incompetence
- **Second-order thinking** — what happens next, and then what happens after that
- **Circle of competence** — know what you know and what you do not
- **Margin of safety** — leave room for being wrong
- **Regression to the mean** — extremes are followed by reversion
- **Survivorship bias** — the losers do not leave records
