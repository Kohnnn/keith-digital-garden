# OpenAI Restructures: The Road from Nonprofit to $852B IPO

OpenAI completed its long-anticipated restructuring in late 2025, converting from a capped-profit nonprofit to a Delaware public benefit corporation. The change sets the stage for a Q4 2026 IPO — directly competing with Anthropic for public market capital.

---

## The Deal Structure

OpenAI Group (the for-profit) is now a PBC owned by a new nonprofit parent called the OpenAI Foundation. The foundation holds a controlling equity stake. Microsoft took 27% ownership as part of the restructuring — formalizing what was previously a revenue-sharing partnership.

Sam Altman received equity for the first time — a ~7% stake valued at roughly $60B at the $852B valuation. The board structure shifted: the nonprofit board retains control over safety decisions, while the for-profit board manages business operations.

## Why Now

Three pressures forced the restructuring: (1) The IPO requires a standard corporate structure — no investor would buy shares in a capped-profit entity. (2) Anthropic's $965B valuation and IPO filing created competitive pressure — OpenAI needed to match the corporate flexibility. (3) Employee liquidity — the restructuring unlocks secondary sales for early employees who've been holding illiquid equity since 2020.

## The Risk

The restructuring opens OpenAI to the same governance problems every for-profit AI company faces: shareholder primacy vs safety mission. The foundation board retains safety veto power, but the history of corporate governance suggests profit incentives eventually dominate.

Anthropic's public benefit corporation structure is similar. Both companies are testing whether you can have a mission-driven AI company that's also publicly traded. The track record is not encouraging.
