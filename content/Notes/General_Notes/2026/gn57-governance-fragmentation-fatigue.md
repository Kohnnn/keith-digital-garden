# Global AI Governance Fragmentation: Audits Are No Longer a One-Off

AI regulation in 2026 is entering an execution phase. EU deadlines are strict, US frameworks are consolidating, and Singapore-style governance expectations are influencing global standards. The result: audit fatigue and audit debt.

---

## What is changing

Regulation is now stack-wide, not feature-wide. The same model can be legal in one jurisdiction and non-compliant in another because obligations differ by risk class, deployment domain, and evidence format.

Teams are learning the same lesson from security compliance: if evidence is not built into CI/CD, it doesn’t exist during regulator review.

## Why audit debt happens

- Overlapping mappings from EU requirements to NIST controls.
- Policy documents that are policy-only, without code-level enforcement.
- Manual evidence collection after incidents instead of immutable telemetry-first workflows.

Audit debt compounds: the longer a team delays remediation, the more expensive every new deployment becomes.

## Practical path

Treat governance like schema migration:
1) canonical control catalog (mapped to each jurisdiction)
2) policy-as-code
3) auto-generated evidence bundles
4) quarterly red-team simulation against controls
