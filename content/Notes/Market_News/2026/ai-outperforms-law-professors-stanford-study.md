# AI outperforms law professors in landmark Stanford study

A Stanford Law study led by Professor Julian Nyarko found that law professors overwhelmingly prefer AI-generated answers to student questions over responses written by their fellow instructors. In blind evaluations of nearly 3,000 comparisons across 16 law professors, AI won 75% of head-to-head matchups. AI responses were flagged as pedagogically harmful only 3.5% of the time, versus 12% for peer-written answers (Stanford Law).

## scene cut
The study is significant because it tested legal reasoning — nuanced, ambiguous, judgment-driven — not factual recall with clear right answers. Previous AI evaluations focused on STEM subjects. Law is the first high-judgment domain where AI demonstrably outperforms domain experts in peer evaluation.

## signal braid
- 16 law professors across US law schools, 40 contract law questions, ~3,000 blind comparisons
- AI won 75% of head-to-head matchups against peer-written answers
- Only 3.5% of AI answers flagged as pedagogically harmful vs 12% for humans
- AI performed comparably to the best human instructor in the study
- Tests used multiple evaluation methods, calibrated response length, and extensive validity checks
- Focus on contract law — requires synthesis of complex material and navigation of ambiguity

## my take
The 3.5% vs 12% harmful flag rate is the most important number. It reverses the default assumption that AI tutoring requires human oversight. The implication for legal education — and by extension any field requiring nuanced reasoning — is that AI is not a supplement. It is the higher-quality baseline.
