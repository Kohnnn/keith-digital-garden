# net liquidity the only macro indicator that matters

Every crypto and equity bull run in the last five years has one thing in common: net liquidity was expanding. Every crash had one thing in common: net liquidity was contracting. Understanding net liquidity — the amount of "real money" available to flow into risk assets — is the single most useful macro skill you can develop.

## the formula

Net Liquidity = Fed Balance Sheet (WALCL) − Treasury General Account (TGA) − Reverse Repo Facility (RRP)

Each component:
- **WALCL** — Total assets held by the Fed. Expands during QE, contracts during QT. The big lever.
- **TGA** — The Treasury's checking account at the Fed. When TGA goes up, money moves from the banking system to the Treasury — reducing liquidity. When TGA goes down (spending), liquidity increases.
- **RRP** — The overnight facility where money market funds park cash at the Fed. When RRP is high, money is sitting on the sidelines. When RRP drains, that money moves into the system.

## how to read it

Net liquidity rising = bullish for risk assets. The Fed creates money, the Treasury spends it, or the RRP releases it — and that money finds its way into markets.

Net liquidity falling = bearish for risk assets. QT absorbs reserves, the Treasury builds cash, or money parks in RRP — and liquidity drains from the system.

The relationship isn't perfectly linear (other factors matter — sentiment, regulation, earnings), but it's the closest thing to a first-order driver of broad market direction that exists.

## the 2023-2024 example

In 2023, the Fed was doing QT (reducing WALCL) while the Treasury was issuing debt (raising TGA). Net liquidity should have collapsed. But RRP was draining at an even faster rate — money market cash was flowing back into the system.

Net liquidity actually rose during QT, sustaining the risk rally. Most macro traders missed this because they only looked at the Fed balance sheet, not the full net liquidity picture.

## my take

I check net liquidity every week. It's the first thing I look at before any macro position. If net liquidity is expanding, I bias toward risk. If it's contracting, I reduce exposure and increase hedges.

The important nuance: net liquidity trends change slowly but turn fast. A quarter of expansion can reverse in two weeks when Treasury refinancing shifts or the RRP floor is hit.

I don't trade based on net liquidity alone. But I don't take a significant macro position without knowing where it stands. It's the gravity that pulls everything else.
