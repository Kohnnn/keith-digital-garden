# RD07: Mass AI Fraud at Brown University — The Detection Gap

A professor at Brown University denounced what they described as “mass AI fraud” on an exam, with hundreds of students using generative AI to complete assignments. The incident highlights the widening gap between AI capability and detection capability.

---

## The incident

Students used AI to generate entire exam responses. The professor detected it not through AI tools but through *stylistic inconsistency* — responses that were structurally perfect but lacked the idiosyncratic errors, reasoning traces, and personal voice that human answers have.

## The detection gap

AI detectors are losing the arms race. Students who use AI strategically (generate outline, then write in their own voice) are undetectable. Students who copy-paste are detectable but not prosecutable at scale.

## The structural problem

Universities are caught between two impossible positions:
1) Ban AI entirely (enforcement impossible, Norway-style)
2) Allow AI entirely (what is the point of an exam?)

The emerging solution: **AI-augmented assessment design**. Write questions that require human judgment, not AI-generatable facts. Test reasoning, not recall. Evaluate process, not output.
