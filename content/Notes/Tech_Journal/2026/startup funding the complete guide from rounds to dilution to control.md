# Startup Funding: The Complete Guide

Four videos synthesised into one comprehensive field guide.

**Sources:**
- YC Brad Flora on fundraising myths (Startup School)
- Rho on dilution mechanics and term sheets
- a16z Speedrun on priced round mechanics
- a16z partners (Horowitz + Andreessen) on post-COVID startup landscape

---

## 1. The Two Paths: Venture-Scale vs Durable Profitable

Every founder makes a choice before they realise they are making it. There are two kinds of companies:

**Venture-scale:** Massive funding rounds, high valuations, eventual IPO or acquisition. Investors bet on 10x-300x returns. Everything about venture is built around that outcome.

**Durable profitable:** Makes money, pays founders well, grows steadily. No outside investors. Founder owns most of it forever.

**The critical mistake:** Taking venture money then deciding you want to run a profitable business at your own pace. You cannot take $5M from a fund built on outlier returns and then slow down. By signing that term sheet, you agree to grow fast enough to exit on the investors' timeline.

**What changed in 2025-2026:** AI collapsed software build costs. Products needing 10 engineers now take one with AI tools. Solo-founded companies went from ~25% of new Carta startups (2019) to over a third today. Skipping funding entirely is more realistic than ever.

## 2. How Funding Rounds Work

Funding comes in stages. No investor writes a check for years of unseen progress.

```
Pre-Seed  -->  Seed  -->  Series A  -->  Series B  -->  Series C+  -->  Exit
   |            |            |             |              |             |
 idea +      product +    $2-3M ARR    $10M+ ARR      scaling      IPO / M&A
 prototype    users
```

### Pre-Seed (2026)
- Under $250K (many) or ~$1.4M average (Carta)
- Mostly SAFEs or convertible notes — no fixed valuation
- Valuation caps typically $10-15M
- AI deals push higher but not dramatically

### Seed
| Metric | 2024 | 2025-2026 |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Median post-money valuation | ~$18M | ~$24M (record) |
| AI premium | ~20% | ~38% higher |
| Typical raise | $2-5M | $2-5M |
| Typical dilution | 15-25% | 15-25% |

### Series A
- Old rule: $1M ARR. No longer enough — now $2-3M
- Exceptions: elite team, exceptional growth, AI angle with competing investors
- Raise: $8-15M. Dilution: 15-25%

### Series B+
- Revenue in tens of millions. Story stops mattering — dashboard is all
- Median founding team ownership by Series B: ~23% (Carta)
- Board can fire founder as CEO

## 3. Fundraising Myths (YC Brad Flora)

### Myth 1: Fundraising is glamorous
**Reality:** Coffee chats, not Shark Tank. One YC company raised $1.6M meeting 160 investors over 4 months and 18 days. 39 said yes. Checks from $5K to $200K. A grind of one-on-one Zoom calls.

### Myth 2: You need money before you start
**Reality:** Build first, get users, then raise. Traction beats pitch decks.

**Case study — Solugen (YC W17):** Chemical manufacturing. Instead of raising $20M for a plant, built a desktop reactor. Then a larger one making hydrogen peroxide to sell. Made $10K/month to hot tub supply stores during the YC batch. Raised $4M seed because they had a working product. Today: $400M+ raised, full-scale plant.

### Myth 3: Your startup must impress
**Reality:** Great startups seem terrible at first. Airbnb = air mattresses on floors. DoorDash = suburban delivery where distances are long. OpenSea = digital collectibles no one could see. Investors want conviction + traction, not polish.

### Myth 4: High valuation = winning
**Reality:** Valuation gets the press release. Terms decide control. See Section 5.

## 4. Dilution Mechanics

The single most misunderstood concept in startup funding.

**How it works:**
- You + co-founder: 100 shares, 50 each (50% ownership each)
- Investor puts in $2M. Company creates 20 new shares for them
- Now 120 shares. You still have 50, but 50/120 = 41.7%
- You sold nothing. Your percentage shrank. That is dilution.

**The Facebook story:** Peter Thiel invested $500K for ~10% in 2004 at <$5M valuation. Company created new shares — founders did not sell. By IPO, every founder owned a fraction of their starting percentage. But the company was worth $100B, so fractions were still billions. That is the trade: percentage for magnitude.

**Key formulas:**
- Dilution = Raise ÷ Post-Money
- Post-Money = Pre-Money + Raise
- Post-Money = Raise ÷ Dilution

Example: $3M seed, 20% dilution. Post-money = $3M / 0.20 = $15M. Pre-money = $15M - $3M = $12M.

**Cumulative dilution:**
| Round | Raise | Post-money | Founder ownership (start: 100%) |
|-------|-------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| Seed | $3M | $15M | ~80% |
| Series A | $10M | $50M | ~64% |
| Series B | $25M | $150M | ~53% |
| Series C | $50M | $400M | ~46% |
| IPO + option pool | — | — | ~23% median (Carta) |

By IPO: Travis Kalanick owned <9% of Uber. Stewart Butterfield ~8% of Slack.

## 5. Term Sheets — What Matters Beyond Valuation

Valuation gets the press release. Terms decide control.

**Liquidation Preference**
- 1x non-participating (founder-friendly): Investors get their money back, done
- Participating (dangerous): Investors get money back + share of remaining proceeds

Example: $10M raised, sold for $30M. Participating means investors take $10M back, then take their ownership % of remaining $20M on top. Founder gets much less than expected.

**Board Structure**
- Founder seats (1-2), Investor seats (1-2), Independent (0-1)
- Investor-controlled board = can fire CEO, block decisions, set comp

**Anti-Dilution**
- Full ratchet: if next round is down, earlier investors' price adjusts. Can destroy founder equity
- Weighted average: gentler. Standard and fair

**Protective Provisions**
Investor veto over: new shares, debt, acquisition, business changes, key hires/fires

**Rule:** Competitive rounds (multiple investors) = cleaner terms. Single-investor rounds = more protection-heavy.

## 6. Valuations — How They Are Really Set

Early-stage valuations are not calculated. They are negotiated.

There is no formula at pre-seed or seed — no revenue, sometimes no product. Valuation becomes a negotiation about risk vs potential return.

**Pre-money vs Post-money (a16z Speedrun):**
- Pre-money: company value before new money
- Post-money: value after adding new money
- Post-money = Pre-money + Raise
- Price per share = Pre-money ÷ existing shares

**The negotiation dynamic:**
| Scenario | Outcome |
|----------|---------|
| Multiple investors competing | Higher valuation, cleaner terms |
| One mildly interested investor | Lower valuation, protection-heavy terms |
| AI startup at seed | ~38% premium |
| Strong traction | Revenue multiple-based valuation |
| No traction, just team | Bet on team quality |

**The biggest trap:** Chasing a high headline valuation with bad terms. A $25M valuation with participating preferred + full ratchet + investor-controlled board is worse than $18M with clean 1x non-participating, weighted average anti-dilution, and founder-controlled board.

## 7. Post-COVID Environment (a16z Partners)

**Trust in Institutions (Andreessen):** COVID was a massive blow to institutional trust. The Gallup trust survey — declining since the 1970s — accelerated sharply post-2020. Both sides (wanted lockdowns sooner vs longer) view what happened as a breach of trust. Result: people want alternatives. Bullish for new startups.

**Remote Work (Horowitz):** Video conferencing went from failed (AT&T Picturephone, 1965) to universal in 2020. Remote/hybrid is here to stay. Hybrid (some in, some remote) is the worst configuration — better to commit fully one way.

**AI Cost Collapse:** AI collapsed software build costs. Solo founders: 25% → 33%+ of new startups. Less capital needed = more leverage in negotiations.

**Operation Warp Speed Model:** Government-as-customer (build this and we buy it) worked for vaccines. Could extend to chips, clean energy, biotech. Political will stalled, but precedent is set.

## 8. Practical Strategy

### Before raising
1. Build something. Get users. Traction beats decks.
2. Know your path (venture vs durable). Do not mix them.
3. Understand SAFEs vs priced rounds.

### During fundraising
- Meet 50-150+ investors
- Expect 15-25% meeting-to-check conversion
- Accept $5K-$200K checks
- Track runway weekly, not quarterly
- Negotiate terms, not just valuation
- Understand board structure

### Key numbers
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Runway target | 18-24 months |
| Dilution per round | 15-25% |
| Median seed post-money 2026 | ~$24M |
| AI premium | ~38% |
| Series A ARR threshold | $2-3M |
| Founder ownership at Series B | ~23% median |
| Meeting-to-check conversion | ~25% |

### Red flags
- Participating liquidation preference
- Full ratchet anti-dilution
- Investor-controlled board at Seed
- Raising before you have users
- Headline valuation with bad terms
