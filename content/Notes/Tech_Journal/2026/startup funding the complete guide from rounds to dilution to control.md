# Startup Funding: The Complete Guide

Four videos synthesised into one comprehensive field guide.

**Sources:**
- YC Brad Flora on fundraising myths (Startup School)
- Rho on dilution mechanics and term sheets
- a16z Speedrun on priced round mechanics
- a16z partners (Horowitz + Andreessen) on post-COVID startup landscape

---

## 1. The Two Paths: Venture-Scale vs Durable Profitable

Every founder makes a choice before they realise they are making it. There are two kinds of companies you can build.

**The venture-scale path:** Massive funding rounds, high valuations, eventual acquisition or IPO. Investors are betting on outlier returns — 10x, 50x, 300x. Everything about venture is built around chasing that outcome.

**The durable profitable path:** Makes money, pays founders well, grows steadily. No outside investors needed. Founder owns most of it forever.

**The critical mistake:** Wanting to build a profitable business you own while taking money from investors who only get paid if the company sells or goes public. You cannot take a M check from a fund built around outlier returns and then decide later you would rather run a profitable business at your own pace. By signing for that money, you agree to grow fast enough to exit on the investors' timeline.

**What changed in 2025-2026:** AI collapsed the cost of building software. Products needing 10 engineers now take one with AI tools. Solo-founded companies went from ~25% of new Carta startups in 2019 to over a third today. Skipping funding entirely is more realistic than ever.

## 2. How Funding Rounds Actually Work

Funding comes in stages. No investor funds years of a company they have not seen progress from yet.

### Pre-Seed (2026)
- Under 50K (many) or ~.4M average (Carta)
- Mostly SAFEs or convertible notes — no fixed valuation yet
- Valuation caps typically 0-15M
- AI deals push higher but not dramatically

### Seed
- Median post-money valuation: ~4M (Carta, record high)
- Up from ~8M the year before
- AI premium: ~38% higher than non-AI
- Typical raise: -5M
- Typical dilution: 15-25%



### Series A
- Old rule: M ARR. No longer enough.
- Investors now want -3M ARR unless growth is exceptional or AI premium applies
- Raise size: -15M
- Dilution: 15-25%

### Series B+
- Revenue in the tens of millions
- Story stops mattering. Dashboard numbers are all that count
- Median founding team ownership at Series B: ~23% (Carta)
- Board can fire founder as CEO

## 3. Fundraising Myths (YC Brad Flora)

### Myth 1: Fundraising is glamorous
**Reality:** It is coffee chats, not Shark Tank. One YC company raised .6M by meeting 160 investors over 4 months and 18 days. 39 said yes. Checks from K to 00K. Fundraising is a grind of one-on-one Zoom meetings.

### Myth 2: You need money before you can start
**Reality:** Build first, get users, then raise. Traction beats pitch decks. Investors want to jump on moving trains.

**Solugen (YC W17):** Chemical manufacturing startup. Instead of raising 0M for a plant, they built a tiny desktop reactor. Then a larger version that made hydrogen peroxide to sell. Made 0K/month selling to hot tub supply stores during the YC batch. Raised M seed because they had a working product. Today: 00M+ raised, full-scale plant.

### Myth 3: Your startup needs to be impressive
**Reality:** Most great startups seem terrible at first. Airbnb was "air mattresses on floors." DoorDash was "suburban food delivery where distances are long." OpenSea was "digital collectibles that only exist on your computer." Investors want conviction and traction, not polish.

### Myth 4: High valuation means you won
**Reality:** Valuation gets the press release. Terms decide who controls the company.

## 4. Dilution Mechanics

The single most misunderstood concept in startup funding.

**How dilution works:**
- You and co-founder start with 100 shares (50 each = 50% ownership each)
- Investor puts in M. Company creates 20 new shares for them
- Now 120 shares total. You still have 50, but 50/120 = 41.7%
- You did not sell a single share. Your percentage shrank. That is dilution.

**The Facebook story:** Peter Thiel invested 00K for ~10% in 2004. Valuation: <M. Company created new shares — founders did not sell any. By IPO, every original founder owned a fraction of their starting percentage, but the company was worth 00B, so the fractions were worth billions. That is the trade: give up percentage for the chance to build something much bigger.

**Key formulas:**
- Dilution = Raise Size / Post-Money Valuation
- Post-Money = Pre-Money + Raise Size
- Post-Money = Raise Size / Dilution

Example: M seed with 20% dilution. Post-money = M / 0.20 = 5M. Pre-money = 5M - M = 2M.

**Cumulative dilution:**
| Round | Raise | Post-money | Approx founder ownership |
|-------|-------|------------|------------------------|
| Seed | M | 5M | ~80% |
| Series A | 0M | 0M | ~64% |
| Series B | 5M | 50M | ~53% |
| Series C | 0M | 00M | ~46% |
| IPO + option pool | — | — | ~23% (Carta median at Series B) |

By IPO, Travis Kalanick owned <9% of Uber. Stewart Butterfield owned ~8% of Slack.

## 5. Term Sheets — What Actually Matters

Valuation gets the press release. Terms decide control.

### Liquidation Preference
| Type | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| 1x non-participating (founder-friendly) | Investors get their money back, nothing more |
| Participating (dangerous) | Investors get money back + share of remaining proceeds |

Example: 0M raised, sold for 0M. Participating preferred means investors take 0M back, then take their ownership % of the remaining 0M on top. Founder gets much less than expected.

### Board Structure
- Founder seats (1-2), Investor seats (1-2), Independent (0-1)
- If investors control the board: they can fire CEO, block decisions, set comp

### Anti-Dilution
- **Full ratchet:** If later round is lower, earlier investors get their price adjusted down. Can destroy founder ownership.
- **Weighted average:** Gentler adjustment. Standard.

### Protective Provisions
Investor veto over: new shares, debt, acquisition, business changes, key hires/fires.

**General rule:** Competitive rounds (multiple investors) = cleaner terms. Single-investor rounds = more protection-heavy.

## 6. Valuations — How They Are Really Set

At early stages, valuations are not calculated. They are negotiated.

There is no formula at pre-seed or seed because there is nothing to plug in — no revenue, sometimes not even a product. Valuation becomes a negotiation about risk vs potential return.

**Pre-money vs post-money (a16z Speedrun):**
- Pre-money: company value before new money
- Post-money: value after new money
- Formula: Post-money = Pre-money + Raise Size
- Price per share = Pre-money / existing shares

**The negotiation dynamic:**
| Scenario | Outcome |
|----------|---------|
| Multiple investors competing | Higher valuation, cleaner terms |
| One mildly interested investor | Lower valuation, protection-heavy terms |
| AI startup at seed | ~38% premium |
| Strong traction | Valuation by revenue multiples |
| No traction, just team | Bet on team quality |

**The biggest trap:** Chasing headline valuation while accepting bad terms. A 5M valuation with participating preferred + full ratchet + investor-controlled board is worse than 8M with clean 1x, weighted average, founder-controlled board.

## 7. Post-COVID Environment (a16z Partners)

### Trust in Institutions
COVID was a massive blow to societal trust in institutions. The Gallup trust survey has been declining since the 1970s but accelerated sharply post-2020. Both sides — those who wanted lockdowns lifted sooner and those who wanted them longer — view what happened as a breach of trust. Result: people want alternatives to existing institutions. Bullish for new startups.

### Remote Work
Video conferencing went from failed (AT&T Picturephone, 1965) to universal in 2020. Remote and hybrid work is here to stay. Hybrid meetings (some in-person, some remote) are the worst configuration — better to go fully one way or the other.

### AI Cost Collapse
The biggest shift for founders: AI collapsed the cost of building software. Solo founders went from 25% to 33%+ of new startups. Less capital needed = more leverage in funding negotiations.

### Operation Warp Speed Model
Government-as-customer ("build this and we will buy it") worked for vaccines. Could apply to chips, clean energy. Political will to extend the model has stalled but the precedent is set.

## 8. Practical Strategy

### Before you raise
1. Build something first. Get users. Traction beats decks.
2. Know which path (venture vs durable). Do not mix them.
3. Understand SAFEs vs priced rounds.

### SAFE route
- Take money now, valuation on conversion at next round
- Valuation cap: 0-15M typical for pre-seed
- No dilution calculation until conversion

### Priced round route
- Set valuation upfront
- Issue preferred shares with specific rights
- Lead investor sets terms, followers accept
- **Strategy:** Secure lead first. They set valuation + terms.

### During fundraising
- Meet 50-150+ investors
- Expect 15-25% meeting-to-check conversion
- Accept K-00K checks
- Track runway weekly, not quarterly
- Negotiate terms, not just valuation
- Understand your board structure

### Key numbers
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Runway target | 18-24 months |
| Dilution per round | 15-25% |
| Median seed post-money 2026 | ~4M |
| AI premium | ~38% |
| Series A ARR threshold | -3M |
| Founder ownership at Series B | ~23% median |
| Meeting conversion rate | ~25% |

### Red flags
- Participating liquidation preference
- Full ratchet anti-dilution
- Investor-controlled board at Seed
- Raising before you have any users
- Valuation way above traction with bad terms
