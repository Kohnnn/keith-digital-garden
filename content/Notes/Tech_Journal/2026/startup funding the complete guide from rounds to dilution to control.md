# Startup Funding: The Complete Guide

Four videos synthesised into one comprehensive field guide.

Sources: YC Brad Flora on fundraising myths, Rho on dilution mechanics and term sheets, a16z Speedrun on priced round mechanics, a16z partners on the post-COVID startup landscape.

---

## 1. The Two Paths: Venture-Scale vs Durable Profitable

Every founder makes a choice before they realise they are making it. There are two kinds of companies you can build.

Venture-scale path: massive funding rounds, high valuations, eventual acquisition or IPO. Investors bet on outlier returns. Durable profitable path: makes money, pays founders well, grows steadily. No outside investors needed.

Critical mistake: wanting to build a business you own while taking money from investors who only get paid if the company exits. You cannot take a $5M check from a fund built around outlier returns and then decide to run a profitable business at your own pace.

What changed in 2025-2026: AI collapsed the cost of building software. Products needing 10 engineers now take one with AI tools. Solo-founded companies went from ~25% of Carta startups in 2019 to over a third today.

## 2. How Funding Rounds Actually Work

Funding comes in stages. No investor funds years of a company they have not seen progress from.

Pre-Seed (2026): Under $250K or ~$1.4M average. Mostly SAFEs or convertible notes. Valuation caps typically $10-15M.

Seed: Median post-money valuation ~$24M (Carta record). Up from ~$18M. AI premium ~38%. Typical raise $2-5M. Dilution 15-25%.

Series A: Old rule $1M ARR no longer enough. Investors want $2-3M ARR unless growth exceptional or AI premium. Raise $8-15M.

Series B+: Revenue in tens of millions. Story stops mattering. Dashboard numbers count. Median founding team ownership at Series B ~23%. Board can fire founder as CEO.

## 3. Fundraising Myths (YC Brad Flora)

Myth: Fundraising is glamorous. Reality: coffee chats, not Shark Tank. One YC company raised $1.6M by meeting 160 investors over 4 months and 18 days. 39 said yes. Checks from $5K to $200K.

Myth: You need money before you start. Reality: build first, get users, then raise. Solugen (YC W17) built a desktop chemical reactor, sold $10K/month of hydrogen peroxide to hot tub stores during batch, raised $4M seed. Today $400M+.

Myth: Your startup needs to be impressive. Reality: Airbnb was air mattresses on floors. DoorDash was suburban food delivery. OpenSea was digital collectibles. Investors want conviction and traction.

Myth: High valuation means you won. Reality: valuation is the press release. Terms decide control.

## 4. Dilution Mechanics

How dilution works: you and co-founder start with 100 shares (50 each = 50%). Investor puts in $2M. Company creates 20 new shares. Now 120 shares total. You still have 50 but 50/120 = 41.7%. Your percentage shrank without selling a share.

Key formula: Dilution = Raise / Post-money. Post-money = Pre-money + Raise. Post-money = Raise / Dilution.

Example: $3M seed, 20% dilution. Post-money = $3M / 0.20 = $15M. Pre-money = $15M - $3M = $12M.

Cumulative dilution by round:

- Seed ($3M, $15M post): ~80% ownership
- Series A ($10M, $50M): ~64%
- Series B ($25M, $150M): ~53%
- Series C ($50M, $400M): ~46%
- IPO + options: ~23% median at Series B

By IPO, Travis Kalanick owned <9% of Uber. Stewart Butterfield owned ~8% of Slack.

## 5. Term Sheets

Liquidation preference: 1x non-participating = investors get money back only. Participating = money back + share of what is left. Example: $10M raised, $30M exit. Participating means investors take $10M then their % of remaining $20M.

Board structure: founder seats (1-2), investor (1-2), independent (0-1). Investor-controlled board can fire CEO, block decisions.

Anti-dilution: full ratchet destroys founder ownership in down rounds. Weighted average is standard.

Competitive rounds = cleaner terms. Single-investor rounds = more protection-heavy.

## 6. Valuations

At early stages valuations are negotiated, not calculated. No revenue, no formula.

Pre-money vs post-money: Pre-money = value before new money. Post-money = value after. Post-money = Pre-money + Raise. Price per share = Pre-money / existing shares.

Biggest trap: chasing headline valuation with bad terms. $25M with participating preferred + full ratchet + investor board is worse than $18M with clean terms.

## 7. Post-COVID (a16z Partners)

Trust in institutions collapsed post-2020. People want alternatives. Bullish for startups.

Remote work went from failed (AT&T Picturephone 1965) to universal. Hybrid meetings are worst configuration.

AI cost collapse: solo founders 25% to 33%+ of new startups. Less capital needed = more leverage.

Operation Warp Speed model: government as customer worked for vaccines. Could extend to chips, clean energy.

## 8. Practical Strategy

Before raising: build something, get users, know your path (venture vs durable).

SAFE route: take money now, valuation set on conversion. Cap $10-15M. Priced round: set valuation upfront, lead sets terms.

During fundraising: meet 50-150+ investors. Expect 25% conversion. Accept $5K-$200K checks. Track runway weekly.

Key numbers: runway 18-24 months, dilution 15-25% per round, median seed post-money $24M, AI premium 38%, Series A threshold $2-3M ARR, founder ownership at Series B ~23%.

Red flags: participating preference, full ratchet, investor board at Seed, raising without users, valuation above traction with bad terms.
