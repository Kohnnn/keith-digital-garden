# a16z Big Ideas 2026: AI in Science, Consumer Apps, and Business Models

Three predictions from a16z partners on where AI is heading in 2026.

---

## 1. Autonomous Labs Accelerate Science (Oliver Shu)

The combination of AI reasoning + robot learning is moving science toward autonomous labs. Near-term: collaborative systems where a scientist works alongside an AI planner + robot executor. Long-term: fully self-driving labs that design experiments, execute them, and iterate without human intervention.

Key areas: life sciences (drug discovery), chemicals, materials science. Startups like Medra (pharma), Chemify (chemistry), Yona Labs are already building. The DOE's Genesis mission brings together academia, national labs, and AI companies.

The big gap is interpretability — scientists need to understand *why* the AI designed an experiment a certain way. Systems that focus on recording every reasoning step will win trust first.

---

## 2. Consumer AI Shifts from Productivity to Connection (Brian Kim)

2026 is the year major consumer AI apps shift from "helping you work" to "helping you connect." Instead of tools that make you more productive, AI that makes you feel seen by others.

The key insight: people are willing to share more of their inner life with AI than with other humans. The next wave uses this to facilitate real human relationships — "my AI talks to your AI" as an opener for conversations we wouldn't have otherwise.

Can startups compete with platforms? Yes — AI introduces net new interaction models that don't natively live in existing social platforms.

---

## 3. AI That Reinforces Business Models Wins (David Haber)

The strongest AI companies aren't cost-reduction stories — they're revenue-reinforcement stories. Two examples:

**Eve (plaintiff law):** AI automates drafting and reasoning for contingency-fee lawyers. They only get paid if they win. AI means they take on more cases and make more money. The business model is reinforced, not eroded.

**Salient (loan servicing):** Voice agents handle collections in 50 languages. Yes, it reduces costs, but more importantly it drives *better collection rates* — directly improving the lender's revenue.

Defensibility: embedding deeply into customer workflows + creating unique outcomes data that model companies can't access from the public internet. Eve processes cases from intake to outcome, building a proprietary dataset nobody else has.
