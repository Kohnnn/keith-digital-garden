# four noble truths eightfold path and the tao of trading

The Four Noble Truths are a debugging protocol for suffering. The Eightfold Path is the fix. The Dao De Jing is a systems-thinking manual. All three are directly applicable to trading.

## four noble truths

1. **Dukkha (unsatisfactoriness):** Trading involves unavoidable discomfort — drawdowns, missed trades, regret. This is not fixable. It is structural.
2. **Samudaya (origin):** The suffering comes from attachment — to being right, to linear equity curves, to past performance.
3. **Nirodha (cessation):** You can stop attaching. The edge exists but doesn't need your ego to validate it.
4. **Magga (the path):** The Eightfold Path below.

## eightfold path mapped to trading

- **Right view:** Understand that markets are non-ergodic, non-stationary, fat-tailed
- **Right intention:** Trade to execute process, not to be right
- **Right speech:** Stop talking about your positions until they're closed
- **Right action:** Execute your plan regardless of emotion
- **Right livelihood:** Trade with capital you can afford to lose
- **Right effort:** Focus on process quality, not P&L
- **Right mindfulness:** Stay present with price action, not P&L
- **Right concentration:** One strategy, one system, one plan per session

## dao de jing for traders

The Dao is not a moral guide. It's a systems design manual. Key lines retranslated for trading:

- "The Dao that can be described is not the eternal Dao" — your model is a map, not the territory
- "Empty the vessel to make it useful" — slack and buffer are features, not bugs
- "The soft overcomes the hard" — trend following flows with the market rather than fighting it
- "Those who know don't speak" — the best traders have the least to say

## yin-yang

Bid and ask. Bull and bear. Volatility and calm. Expansion and contraction. These are not oppositions — they are complementary forces that create each other. A trend cannot exist without a range to break from. A crash cannot occur without a prior rally. Trading the polarity rather than fighting one side is the Dao approach.
