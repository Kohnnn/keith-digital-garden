# mathematicians issue warning — AI and the nature of expertise

A group of mathematicians published a warning in Science as AI rapidly gains ground in theorem-proving and mathematical reasoning. The core concern is not that AI will replace mathematicians — it is that the nature of mathematical knowledge itself changes when a machine can produce results no human fully understands.

## the warning

The concern is epistemological. Mathematics has traditionally been a domain where proof equals understanding. A theorem is not just a true statement — it is a statement accompanied by a chain of reasoning that a human can follow, validate, and internalize. AI-generated proofs break that chain. They may be correct. But if no human can reconstruct the reasoning, is it still mathematics?

This is not a hypothetical. AI systems have already produced novel mathematical results. Some have been verified. Some have not. The gap between machine-generated truth and human comprehension is growing.

## what this means for expertise

The Stanford Law study showing AI outperforming professors and the mathematicians' warning are two sides of the same coin. In law, the reaction is cautious optimism — AI as better tutor. In mathematics, the reaction is alarm — AI as fundamentally different way of knowing.

The difference is that law is about persuasion and judgment. Mathematics is about proof and understanding. In law, a correct answer that no one can fully explain is still useful. In mathematics, a correct theorem that no one understands is incomplete — the understanding is the point.

## my take

I think the mathematicians are right to be concerned, but the genie is not going back in the bottle. The question is not whether AI-produced mathematics counts as knowledge — it is whether we develop new epistemic standards for evaluating machine-generated results. Verification may replace proof. Empirical testing of mathematical claims may supplement deductive reasoning. The nature of expertise shifts from "can produce the result" to "can evaluate the result." That is a harder skill, but it is still a human one.
