# weekly market report 2026 w25 nfp week and the iran peace trade

The week ended with two macro risks reinforcing each other. The initial May payroll estimate showed 172,000 jobs added and unemployment unchanged at 4.3 percent ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_06052026.htm)). At the same time, renewed Middle East tension weakened hopes for a near-term U.S.-Iran settlement, while the strong jobs print pushed investors toward higher-for-longer rate expectations ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-06-05/)).

*see also:* [[weekly-market-report-2026-W24]] · [[weekly market report 2026 w26 nfp beats 172k but labor market is freezing]]

## scene cut

The peace trade was always conditional. Lower oil and stronger risk assets depended on de-escalation, while softer labor data was supposed to preserve room for rate cuts. By Friday, the labor surprise and renewed conflict risk had challenged both sides of that setup ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-06-05/)).

## signal braid

- May payrolls initially rose by 172,000, compared with 179,000 in April, while unemployment held at 4.3 percent ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_06052026.htm)).
- The two-year Treasury yield reached 4.147 percent after the report as markets increased the probability of tighter Fed policy ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-06-05/)).
- The Dow fell 1.4 percent, the S&P 500 lost 2.64 percent, and the Nasdaq fell 4.2 percent in the June 5 session ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-06-05/)).
- Brent settled 2 percent lower at $93.09 and WTI fell 2.69 percent to $90.54, but both contracts still recorded weekly gains ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-06-05/)).
- Bitcoin fell 3.88 percent to about $61,157 and was headed for a weekly decline of nearly 18 percent ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-06-05/)).

## my take

This was not a clean growth-positive jobs report for markets. Stronger payrolls reduced the immediate case for easing just as oil and conflict risk kept pressure on inflation expectations. The useful signal was the interaction: rate sensitivity and geopolitical sensitivity were no longer offsetting each other.
